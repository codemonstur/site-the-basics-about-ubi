The way you initially calculated it, the cost would be 1 dollar a month, or perhaps 12 dollars per year. Because in your calculation, you looked only at the money flowing out. But look at it from the perspective of the bank account, whatever comes in as a payment was taken out as a tax before, the transactions always cancel each other out. Now let’s raise this basic income to a million dollars. Every month you see a million dollars being removed from your account to be followed by another transfer adding a million dollars. What is the cost of the system? Again in your original calculation you only looked at money going out so it would be 12 million dollars a year! For one person! … yes it would, so what? All outgoing transactions are matched with incoming ones. What if we were to extend this basic income to apply to all inhabitants of the country? Let’s say we have 1 million inhabitants. What is the cost? Oh my god, 12 trillion dollars! Does that matter? It’s a big number, but we are still creating the same 0 dollar net outcome in all the bank accounts of all the inhabitants.

Now let’s look at it from another perspective.

Imagine a group of people in a country. The country has some homes, some water, some food, some clothes, and some unused labor potential because they are all just sitting around in a circle. The inhabitants also all have a bank account with some money in it. Now you turn on your basic income system. What is the end result? Effectively not much. The country still exists, there is the same amount of water, same amount of food, same amount of clothes, everybody is still just sitting around in a circle, so the labor potential is still the same. Only the money has been rearranged a bit. The real cost here is only a little bit of electricity to run the computers to move the money around. Nothing else has changed.

So what is a cost? A cost is someone somewhere doing actual work. A cost is something of value being consumed. In many cases, a financial transaction involves getting a thing. Like when you buy a sandwich. We can assume that you will eat that sandwich, and it will be lost. The transaction is a proxy for the consumption that is about to happen. A basic income payment on the other hand does not involve consumption. It is what economists call a transfer payment.

There is a reason I said your argument was obviously flawed. Transfer payments not being costs is standard economic theory that you get taught in economics class. Here is the Wikipedia article on transfer payments, which states in its opening paragraph: “These payments are considered to be non-exhaustive because they do not directly absorb resources or create output.

In case you are wondering, if all payments always balance, what is the point of having the system in the first place? And the answer is simple, because you can mess around with the tax and create a differential between how much is going in and how much is going out for an individual account. So one person can receive more than he is paying, and another can pay more than he is receiving.

The only things you have to worry about with a transfer payment system is fairness and the incentives to work. Most of the stuff we have, has to be made by someone. And that someone isn’t going to like having to work while others just receive for free. But here a basic income works very well. The basic income always goes to everyone, including the guy that works. So as an individual you don’t have to worry about not getting something others are getting, you get that payment too. All you have to do is decide if your salary is worth doing the job.

And in case you are wondering, wouldn’t everyone just quit their jobs and never work again? No, of course not, because if people would do that, the amount of stuff in the country decreases, the value of the basic income goes down because the price of products increases. With the basic income not covering your cost of living, you would quickly go find a job to supplement your income. The market will find a balance.
